Read fights.md. It holds the "what people are fighting about" items from this morning's tech digest and, for each one, the post behind it, what that post responds to, and the replies and quote posts that came from the accounts I follow. The file's header says what is and is not in it: replies from people outside those accounts are not there, so the "N of M" counts tell you how much of the conversation you can actually see.

You are writing the "dive deeper" panel that opens under each of those digest items, for one person: a web developer who also follows AI closely. Same voice as the digest: plain, simple language, like explaining to a smart friend over coffee. Short sentences. Explain a technical term in a few words the first time it appears.

Searching: you have WebSearch, for one thing only. When an item is about something the reader may not know (a product, a project, a technique with a name, a benchmark, a person's role) and fights.md does not explain it, search to find out what it is, how it differs from what came before, and what people use it for. At most two searches per item, none when the file already explains it. Never search for opinions, reactions or "what people are saying": that part comes only from fights.md. If a search does not settle what the thing is, say you could not find out rather than guessing.

Structure:

# 🔍 Deeper on <date>

Then one "### " section per item in fights.md, in the same order, in exactly this shape:

### <the item's headline, copied verbatim from fights.md, emoji included>
One or two sentences: what the fight is really about, in one breath. Then the post's URL on its own line, copied verbatim from fights.md.

https://x.com/...

#### 🧭 What it is
Only when the item needs it. Two to five sentences: what the thing is, how it differs from what came before, what it is for in practice. If you searched, start with "From a search:" so the reader knows it did not come from the thread.

#### ⚔️ The camps
The two to four positions people are taking. One paragraph per position, with a blank line between paragraphs: the position in bold, one sentence on the claim, then the strongest line for it, quoted or closely paraphrased, with who said it (@handle) and how many likes it got. The original poster's own position is one of the camps. When the visible replies and quote posts are few, say so in a paragraph of its own first ("Only N of the M replies are from accounts I follow, so this is a partial view.") rather than inventing camps.

#### 🌱 Spin-offs
Only when a quote post started a conversation of its own (it has replies or quote posts of its own in the file). One "##### " block per spin-off:

##### @handle: what their post argues, in one line
One or two sentences: what they added and what it drew in reply. Then that post's URL on its own line.

https://x.com/...

#### 🤔 So what
One or two sentences: what to take from this, and which side has the better argument if one does. Say it is unclear when it is.

Omit "What it is" and "Spin-offs" when there is nothing for them; never omit "The camps" or "So what". For an item with no conversation data (an article, not a post), write "What it is" and "So what" only, from the digest's words and, if needed, a search.

Start your final message with the "# " title line. No sentence before the title. End with the last item's "So what": no closing line, no sign-off, no attribution or co-author line.
