radar.md above is what the reader's ideas radar holds: problems that small business owners in the UK and the US keep raising, each with its counts (worked out by code, so trust them) and, when it has one, its verdict on whether it would make a business for a small team: Worth testing, Watch or Pass, with its scores, reason and smallest test. It is grouped by what is new since the reader's last brief.

The reader is a software developer who builds with a couple of friends and AI, looking for a problem to build a paid product for. They asked for this brief instead of reading every problem, so tell them in a minute what deserves their attention and why.

- `summary`: the gist in two to four plain sentences. Lead with the strongest thing; if little changed since the last brief, say so plainly rather than dressing it up.
- `items`: at most 12, in the groups the schema names. "Worth a look" is for the problems most worth their time now, Worth testing first, then a Watch with strong or growing evidence. "New and rising" is for problems that appeared or picked up since the last brief and look like more than noise. "Verdicts that changed" says what moved and why. "Your starred" says what new evidence the starred problems got. Each line says why this problem is here, with the numbers that matter; don't repeat its name, the page shows it. Leave out problems with nothing to say.
- `next`: the one thing worth doing next.

Use only what radar.md says; never add facts, counts or competitors it doesn't have. Plain words, no hype.

Deliver the brief by calling the StructuredOutput tool with it as the arguments (`items` an array, not a string). Don't write it or any JSON as text.
