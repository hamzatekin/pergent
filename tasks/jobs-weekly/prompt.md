market.md above holds the facts of the last seven days about the UK job market for software developers, counted by code from the adverts collected every day: volume by role, sponsorship, work mode, pay and what it is worth in Nottingham after tax and rent, the most asked-for skills, AI coding tools, and a shortlist of every open advert at a sponsor that fits the reader, however long ago it was first seen. previous.md lists what the last two papers said.

The reader is a senior frontend engineer living in Nottingham who builds with React and TypeScript (and React Native and Expo), is open to full stack and backend roles but not management, and needs an employer that sponsors a Skilled Worker visa. The paper comes every morning: their five-minute read on where the market is going and what to apply for. The week's figures roll, so say "in the last seven days", not "this week".

Write the paper:

- `title`: "Job market, <the date in market.md's heading>".
- Section "Last seven days": a `lead` of three to five sentences with the main numbers (open adverts, new ones, the share at sponsors, the frontend side's share), then up to three stories (importance 2) on what stands out: a role, skill, sponsorship or remote share that moved, or in the first week, what the baseline shows.
- Section "Frontend side": what frontend, full stack and mobile adverts ask for: their skills, pay, remote and office share, years asked and sponsorship, against the market as a whole. Two to four stories, plus one-line stories (importance 1) for smaller points.
- Section "Pay where you'd live": what pay is worth in Nottingham after tax and rent, place by place, from market.md's table: which places pay more once rent is taken off, and what London pay is really worth. One or two stories.
- Section "Skills": the skills that lead and, when market.md compares weeks, the ones that rose or fell. Say what it means for someone with the reader's stack. Two or three stories, one-line stories (importance 1) for the rest.
- Section "Worth applying": from the shortlist, the five to eight adverts that fit the reader best (the shortlist is already ordered by fit; an advert marked new since yesterday goes first among equals), each a story (importance 2): `headline` "<role> at <company>", `body` two or three sentences on why it fits and the main gap (the fit's reason, its stack against theirs, remote or hybrid, pay and what it is worth in Nottingham when given, what the advert says about sponsorship), `source` the advert's URL exactly as given. Then every other advert of the shortlist as a one-line story (importance 1) in the same `stories` array, each with its `source`, so the section is the whole list of what is open and worth a look. When the shortlist is empty, say so in the section's `lead` and leave `stories` empty.

Every number comes from market.md, as written there. Never work out a new figure, never guess at a trend the tables do not show, and when market.md says there is no earlier week, say this is the baseline instead of calling anything a rise or a fall. Use `context` sparingly, for what a number means for the reader; the page already labels it "What it means for you", so don't start it with those words. Every story of a section, one-line ones too, goes in its `stories` array; a section has no other list. Keep it plain: short sentences, no hype, no advice on CVs or interviews.

Deliver the paper by calling the StructuredOutput tool once. Write each text field as one JSON string, with a line break written as \n, never a raw new line inside a string. Call it with the paper as the arguments (`sections` an array, not a string). Don't write the paper or any JSON as text.
